Part 8 · Start here

Relationships & social life

Close relationships are among the strongest predictors of health and happiness. The good news: the skills are learnable.

Educational, not therapy. These ideas come from relationship science and clinical psychology. If social anxiety, low mood or a relationship is affecting your life, a GP or psychologist can help, and effective treatments exist.

🚨 If you're not safe: control, threats, monitoring, isolation or violence are abuse, not a communication problem. Contact the UK National Domestic Abuse Helpline (0808 2000 247) or US National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233), or local emergency services. If you're thinking of harming yourself: Samaritans 116 123 (UK) or 988 (US).

A relationship checklist

Small, evidence-backed habits.

👀 Turn toward bidsNotice small attempts to connect and respond, even briefly.
🎉 Celebrate good news activelyAsk questions and help people relive it, before any worries.
🙂 Assume you're likedPeople like you more than you think after a conversation (the liking gap).
⏳ Invest the hoursFriendship takes time: repeated, unplanned contact and shared activities.
🪜 Climb, don't avoidFace social fears in small steps, and drop safety behaviours.
🏷️ Name the feelingPrecise labels calm emotions; reappraisal beats suppression.
🧱 Say it clearlyDescribe, express, specify, consequences. Kind and firm can go together.
🛠️ Repair quicklyApologise with responsibility and an offer to fix it; take breaks when flooded.
