Communication & the pattern lab

Skills that compound in any career: getting your meaning across, negotiating and persuading well, and reading rooms well enough to predict what will happen in them.

Holmes told Watson: “You see, but you do not observe.” In corporate life the same gap separates people who are surprised by every meeting from people who walk in knowing who will object, what will persuade them and who actually decides. That isn't magic. It's pattern recognition built from deliberate observation, written notes, predictions and honest scoring. This part gives you the principles of communication, negotiation and persuasion, then a working Meeting Pattern Lab to practise them with your own meetings.

Part I · 21 ideas

🗣️ First principles of communication

Communication fails by default: meaning is lost in encoding, noise and decoding, and what you meant is not what they heard. These are the principles that survive every medium, from a one-line Slack reply to a board memo.

Part II · 22 ideas

🤝 Negotiation

Every job involves negotiating: budgets, deadlines, salaries, scope, vendors. The research-backed core is surprisingly simple: know your alternatives, look for the interests behind positions, create value before you claim it, and remember you'll meet again.

The lab

🧪 The Meeting Pattern Lab

Observe → record → find patterns → predict → check → update. Holmes's method, as a working tool for your own meetings.

1 · ObserveWho speaks last? Who do people glance at before agreeing? What question stalls things?
2 · RecordLog each meeting within the hour: stances, objections, whose view won, what tactics were used.
3 · MineThe lab surfaces influence, objection maps and which tactics work, with a confidence warning when data is thin.
4 · PredictBefore the next meeting: who will object, why, who to pre-wire first and the chance of approval.
5 · ScoreAfterwards, record what happened. Your Brier score shows whether your “read” is improving.

Before your next meeting

A one-page checklist.

🎯 One messageWhat is the single thing they must remember? Put it first (BLUF), with the intent behind it.
👂 Their frame, not yoursWhat does each person care about and fear? Translate your point into their terms.
🗺️ Map the roomWho decides formally? Who sways informally? Who is likely to object, and about what?
🤝 Pre-wireTalk to the likely objectors and the real influencers one-on-one first. Meetings ratify decisions made before them.
🔮 Predict and write it downNote your forecast: outcome, objections, surprises. You can't improve a judgement you never recorded.
👀 Observe, don't just seeIn the room, watch reactions, glances, silences and who speaks last, not just the slides.
🔁 Close the loopSummarise decisions and owners aloud, then in writing. Ask “what did you hear?” when it matters.
📓 Log and scoreWithin an hour, log what happened in the Pattern Lab and score your prediction.
