## Slide 13: We've thought about what could go wrong

- The platform becomes a bottleneck → Self-service first
- It becomes a gatekeeper, not a service → Measure the platform by customers' speed and satisfaction
- Cognitive load is hard to measure → Use proxies: lead time, number of tools and steps to production, time spent on plumbing, maker survey scores. Measure before and after onboarding.
- Banks fund projects, not long-lived teams → Fund the platform as a standing product team
- Reorganising is disruptive → No reporting-line changes for value streams. Only the platform team is new

## Slide 15: Appendix: questions and answers

- Will we lose control of our apps and roadmap? No. You keep owning your apps, requirements, backlog and priorities.
- Can we still experiment and try new things? Yes, in sandbox environments with lighter rules, and new connectors can be requested through a fast route.
- Do our makers have to move into the central team? No. Makers stay in your value stream; the central team is small and focused on foundations.
- What if a block doesn't fit our need? Extend it, ask for a change, or build your own with a short justification.
- Won't this slow us down? It shouldn't, and we'll measure it: lead time to production is a headline metric.
- How long will onboarding take? Typically a few weeks to land critical apps, with blocks adopted as apps are next changed.
- What if the platform team is busy when we need something? Most needs are self-service; for the rest, there's reserved onboarding capacity and published service levels.
- Who pays for the platform? The core platform is centrally funded at first; premium or dedicated services may be charged later, with costs visible to each value stream.
