Part 12 · Start here

Numbers & data

Numbers are how the world argues. Reading them well is a superpower; reading them badly is how you get fooled.

You don't need maths beyond school to think clearly about data. You need a handful of questions: compared with what? Out of how many? How was the sample chosen? Could something else explain it? Is this a big effect or just a significant one? This part builds those habits with interactive examples.

Recap

Questions to ask any number

A pocket checklist.

🤔 Compared with what?A number alone means little; find the baseline.
🔢 Out of how many?Turn percentages into natural frequencies.
🎯 Average of what?Mean or median? What's the spread?
🧺 Who was counted?How was the sample chosen, and who is missing?
📏 How big is it?Significant isn't the same as important.
🔁 Has it replicated?One surprising study is a hypothesis.
🔀 What else could explain it?Confounders, selection, reverse causation.
📉 Is the chart honest?Check axes, scales and cherry-picked ranges.
