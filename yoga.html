Yoga

Yoga is a family of practices for body, breath and attention, with deep roots and a modern evidence base.

⚕️ Educational, not medical advice. Yoga complements, never replaces, medical treatment. If you have axSpA, osteoporosis, scoliosis, heart or eye conditions, or are pregnant, check with your clinician or physiotherapist about which practices suit you.

Most people meet yoga as a stretching class, but the word covers a 2,000-year-old family of philosophies and practices: ethics, postures, breathing, concentration and meditation. This part covers all of it. It respects the traditions, is honest about what the research does and doesn't show (strong enough for back pain, promising for mood, balance and sleep, not a cure for anything), and pays close attention to practising safely with axSpA, scoliosis, osteoporosis, hypermobility or fatigue.

A yoga checklist

Practising well, for life.

🐢 Little and oftenTen minutes most days beats one long class a week.
🧱 Use propsBlocks, straps, chairs and walls make poses fit your body.
🚫 No forcingStretch sensation is fine; sharp, pinching or electric pain means stop.
🦒 Protect the neckSkip headstand and shoulderstand with a stiff or fused neck, osteoporosis or high blood pressure.
🦴 Hinge, don't roundWith osteoporosis, fold with a long spine and favour back-extension.
🌬️ Breathe gentlySlow, quiet nasal breathing; forceful breathing isn't for everyone.
🛋️ Flare days countRestorative poses or five minutes of breathing are real practice.
🗣️ Tell your teacherMention axSpA, scoliosis or anything else; ask your physio what suits you.
