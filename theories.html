The theory map

Why learn theories at all? Because a good theory compresses a mountain of facts into one idea you can carry anywhere.

The investor Charlie Munger called it a latticework of mental models. If you know only one theory, every problem looks like it fits that theory. If you know dozens from different fields, you can look at the same situation through several lenses and see what one-lens thinkers miss. The theories here were chosen because they are deep (they explain a lot with little), transferable (useful far beyond their home field) and timely (they matter for a world shaped by AI, networks, geopolitics and fast change).

Each card gives you the core idea, why it gives you an edge now, a concrete example, the most common misconception, and links to connected theories. Search, filter by domain, or let chance pick. Your progress is saved in this browser.

A theory is not a fact. It's a model: a simplified story that makes predictions. Some here are among the best-tested ideas humans have produced (relativity, evolution, thermodynamics). Others are influential but contested (Sapir–Whorf, the Great Man theory, the Thucydides Trap). Each card tells you which is which. Knowing a theory's limits is part of knowing the theory.

How to think in models

Collecting theories is the easy part. Here's how to use them.

🔭 Use several lensesFor any big decision, ask how three or four theories from different domains would explain the situation. Where they agree, you can be confident. Where they disagree, that's where you need to look harder.
🎯 Start from base ratesBayes: before the vivid story, ask how often this kind of thing happens in general. Then update as evidence arrives.
🔁 Look for feedback loopsSystems thinking: most surprises come from loops and delays, not from villains.
📈 Check the distributionIs it a bell curve or a power law? Averages mislead in fat-tailed worlds such as wealth, pandemics, startups and wars.
♟️ Think about incentivesGame theory and principal–agent: what does each player gain from each move, and what happens when the game repeats?
🧬 Variation + selectionEvolution explains more than biology: markets, ideas, memes and code all evolve.
🧠 Distrust your first answerSystem 1 is fast and confident. Prospect theory, dissonance and bounded rationality show where it goes wrong.
❌ Try to prove yourself wrongPopper: state in advance what would falsify your belief. Kuhn: notice the anomalies your field ignores.
🗺️ The map is not the territoryEvery theory simplifies. Know its assumptions and where it stops working.
