⚕️ Educational, not medical advice. This page summarises mainstream evidence and guidelines to help you understand your health and have better conversations with your clinicians. It can't know your history. Don't start, stop or change any treatment without your doctor, rheumatologist, allergist or pharmacist.

🚨 Get urgent help for: a painful red eye with light sensitivity or blurred vision (possible uveitis: same-day eye care); swelling of the lips or tongue, trouble breathing or feeling faint after an allergen (anaphylaxis: use an adrenaline autoinjector if prescribed and call emergency services); back pain with numbness around the groin or new bladder or bowel problems (emergency); sudden weakness, facial droop or speech problems (stroke: emergency); thoughts of harming yourself (UK Samaritans 116 123 · US 988 · or your local emergency number).

A weekly health checklist

The highest-evidence habits, on one page.

😴 Protect sleepRegular times, morning light, caffeine before noon if you're foggy, alcohol low. Track hours: you can't feel sleep debt.
🧘 Move dailyA short mobility routine (extension, rotation, breathing) every day, plus 150+ minutes of activity and two strength sessions a week, adapted with your physio.
🪑 Change position oftenThe best posture is the next one. Break up sitting every 30–60 minutes.
💊 Treat the disease properlyTake treatment as agreed, track BASDAI, and ask for a review if it stays at 4 or more. Don't smoke.
🤧 Control allergies, not your brainRegular nasal steroid if advised; prefer non-drowsy antihistamines; ask about immunotherapy if uncontrolled.
📝 Offload your memoryCapture tasks in one place, write meeting cue cards, and use "let me think for a second" when you go blank.
🧠 Look after your mindKeep up connection, notice low mood early, and ask for psychological support: it's part of treating a chronic illness.
🩺 Bring dataTwo weeks of logs and your top concern make short appointments count.
